# Musing on Determining AI Readiness: Design Principles for a Diagnostic that works

Here are my 5 starters. Did I get it right? Wrong? What's your advice to improve on AI Readiness assessment and reporting?

1. **Autonomous tests over self-reporting.** Measure what an AI agent encounters by directly probing and testing the live setup. Remove the HITL (Human-in-the-Loop) from the measurement process as much as possible, and you remove the largest source of inflation before it starts.

2. **Diagnose to guide, not to grade.** The assessment exists to find gaps and route the right effort to the right places. A pretest, not a final mark. Fix what's worth fixing; that differs for everyone and only means something in context.

3. **Measure substance, not presence.** Existence isn't readiness, and volume isn't value. A signal counts only if the thing actually works for its purpose, ideally against a real reference rather than arbitrary coherence.

4. **Keep it open and reproducible.** Anyone should be able to run the test and verify the numbers. Openness is what keeps it honest, but the numbers need to be interpreted in the right context to be meaningful.

5. **Minimize the burden; avoid performative metrics theater.** Make reporting simple without the friction. If the instrument becomes 'yet another reporting exercise,' it will cost thousands of hours and provide less meaningful results.
