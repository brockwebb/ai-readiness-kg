**Leg states, per body.** Each leg is in one of three states:
- **clear:** at least one judged row, every judged row passed, and no `error` row. A leg whose every row is `not_applicable` also counts as clear.
- **fail:** at least one `fail` row.
- **unobservable:** no fail, but an `error` row, another non-judged verdict, or no row at all.

**The walk.** Criteria are taken in the record's order, and the walk stops at the first one that is not all clear. A fail there gives level k-1. With no fail there, the level is also k-1 and is reported as `k-1 (unobservable at k)`. Every output prints the per-criterion clear / fail / unobservable counts the level is read from: the grid prints clear/legs, and `--body` and `--json` print all three.

**The rubric, as generated** (one sentence per level):

- Level 0: every body holds it. A body stays here while any scored leg of criterion A fails; one whose criterion A legs show no fail but an error or no row is reported as '0 (unobservable at 1)'.

## 4. Every premise this task file got wrong

1. **"Level names are the criterion titles from the record"** paired with the gloss **"A discoverability and access, then B semantics, then D terms, then F stability"**. The record's names are `ACCESSIBLE`, `UNDERSTANDABLE`, `OPEN` and `release engineering` (`crit:*.name`). The ladder uses the record's names, as the decision requires. The gloss is not the record.
2. **"W3C DWBP (on disk) cites the Open Data Barometer and the five-star open data deployment scheme (Berners-Lee, 2010…)"**. DWBP cites the ODB (`[ODB]`). It does not cite the five-star scheme: it mentions "the 5 Stars of Linked Data" once in BP 10's prose, with no reference entry and no statement of the stars. The five-star scheme is cited by reference only.
3. **"reported as `≤ k-1 (unobservable at k)`"**. The `≤` is the wrong direction. Reaching criterion k means every criterion below it was clear, so the body demonstrably holds k-1, and what is unknown is whether it holds k or more. Its true level is therefore ≥ k-1. The report is `k-1 (unobservable at k)`: the placement is the floor, and the next level is marked unobserved. It is never rounded up, which is what the decision protects, and the test asserts no body is placed at or above a criterion where it has an error.
4. **"passes outright (the gating view's 'outright' count, per criterion)"**. The gating view counts a leg as outright when every **judged** row passed, and ignores `error` rows. Decision 3's own error rule cannot use that count. NCHS's A3 is `{pass: 1, error: 1}`: outright in the gating view (2/14), but unobservable on the ladder (A clear 1/10). The ladder counts **clear** legs (outright with no error row) and prints them as their own column. The gating count is unchanged.
5. **"If it is met, promote A12 … the scored-leg count moves from 14 to 15"**. The criterion is not stated in terms the record can evaluate, so this branch could not be taken (§2). Separately, the adopted-harness-leg **denominator** is now 23, not 16, because pre-registered rules have been added since the scoring-model task (§1). The scored count is 14.
6. **"Levels are built by criterion order as the framework already orders them"**. The record has no explicit order field on criteria. The order used is the record's criterion codes, A to G, which `framework_totals` already sorts, restricted to criteria with a scored leg. This is the reading the task's own gloss gives (A, B, D, F).
7. **"Expected skips: 3"**. Correct; they are the three listed in §5.
