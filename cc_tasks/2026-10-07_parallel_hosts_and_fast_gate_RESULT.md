## 2. What changed
- `manners.Fetcher`: one re-entrant lock per netloc, held from the robots gate through the request and its backoff. It guards the clock, the robots cache and the request count. A bookkeeping lock is never held across I/O. Result: one request in flight per host, the Heritrix setting.
- `run.map_by_host`: one worker per surface netloc. `run_cycle` and `run_controls` use it (each fixture is its own server, so its own netloc). Groups are submitted largest first (Graham's LPT) and results are reassembled in input order. `manners.max_parallel_hosts`: null means one worker per netloc, capped at 16. A value of 1 is the old loop. A value outside 1..16 is refused.
- Guards (`tests/test_parallel_hosts.py`, in `make guards`): a serial and a parallel cycle over 4 loopback servers give payloads equal after removing timing fields. The timing removed is `captured_at`, `read_at`, `elapsed_ms`, the HTTP `Date` header and E5's quoted stamp; `robots_log` order is compared as a set. The control gate's verdicts are also equal serial and parallel. Gaps per netloc are at least 1/rate on both the virtual and the real clock, including a host two workers share. Positive controls: a flipped verdict breaks the equality, and the unlocked Fetcher breaks the gap.
