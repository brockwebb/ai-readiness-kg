**What is scored.** The harness has 17 `harness_leg` indicators. 16 are adopted and 1 is a candidate (A12, DD-054). 14 of the 16 adopted ones are scored:
- **E5** is on no published matrix. It judges this instrument's own controls, and its actions carry `applies_to_publisher: false`.
- **G1-D** was withdrawn by DD-066, effective cycle 5. It is not a leg of the cycle of record.
- **A12** is structured but never scored.

The design page tables all 17 with the reason for each. The scored legs sit under 4 of the 7 criteria (A, B, D, F); C, E and G have no scored leg. The totals follow the record's own DD-054 split: 48 indicators and 47 constructs, the same as `counts`, and a test asserts the agreement.

**Finding 1: the three bodies the harness cannot reach go unscored, and only the candidate leg sees them.** For BLS, BTS and ORES, every one of the 14 scored rows is `error`. Their only judged verdict on the cycle of record is **A12 = fail**, on the candidate leg. The model does what decision 3 says: `error` leaves the denominator, and with nothing left there is no score. The result is that the additive model says nothing about exactly the bodies where the instrument's loudest signal is. The model should not work around this by counting `error` as fail, because that would be imputing a verdict (Handbook step 3). The grid already prints `0/14` and `err 14` for these bodies. What would close the gap is adopting A12, and that decision already belongs to the operator (`ind:A12.candidate_promotion`). This task changes nothing about it.

**Finding 2: hierarchical equal weights are very unequal weights per leg.** For a body with full coverage, each of the 10 criterion-A legs carries 1/10 × 1/4 = **1/40** of the score. D1 and D4 carry **1/8** each, and B3 and F4 carry **1/4** each. So:
- BEA and NCSES tie for first **only because B3 passes** (B = 1.000 against 0.000 elsewhere).
- A body's A score, the criterion with the most measurement behind it, can move its total by at most 0.25.

This is the property the Handbook warns about: equal weights at one level are implicit weights at the level below. Decision 3 stands, because no documented basis for other weights exists. The design page's step 6 names what would justify a departure: a stated priority from the working group, or an empirical relation between a leg and use of the data, recorded before the weight is set. Until then, the grid prints the per-criterion columns beside the total, as the Open Data Barometer and ODIN do.

**Finding 3: F carries no information on this cycle.** F4 fails for every scored body, so F = 0.000 in every row and dropping it moves no rank (§3). It still lowers every total by the same fraction, which is why no body scores above 0.35.

**Gating view.** "Outright" counts the legs where every judged row passed. At most 5 of 14 legs pass outright for any body (BEA, NCSES). Every scored body has at least five constructs with zero passes, and 12 of the 13 have at least nine. The first zero construct in framework order is *Machine-readable formats* (A1) for 12 of the 13, and *Programmatic access* for EIA, whose A1 row is `error`. **An additive 0.35 sits on top of nine empty constructs.** Decision 4 exists so that a reader can see exactly that.

## 2. The prescription join

**Ordering.** Effort bands are ordinal (`hours < days < weeks < quarter`). Dividing a score change by an ordinal is not defined (Stevens 1946), so "delta per effort band" is implemented lexicographically: cheapest band first, then the largest score change within the band. The band is printed on every row, so a reader can re-sort.

**What delta means.** A delta is the body's score if every failing row on that leg passed. The matrices record verdicts, not outcomes, so every action on the same leg gets the same delta, and that delta is an upper bound for any single action. This is the same caveat `bodies_failing_now` carries on the record. `test_every_prescription_delta_is_a_rescore` checks every printed delta against a rescore of the flipped cells.
