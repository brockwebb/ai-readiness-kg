## 1. The decision

Page two of the executive summary is the instrument table, with the agency grid beneath it. The finding page two carries is what the framework can and cannot yet defensibly measure, not a pass/fail tally.

The grid's thirteen checks by audit state: `specified` A1, A3, A5, D4; `invalid` A2, A4, A6; `project_defined` A8, A9, A10, B3, D1, F4. A11-declared, A12 and A13 are outside the grid.

The three options the audit left open, and why this one:

- A grid alone over the four `specified` checks (64 cells: 41 could not measure, 15 fail, 8 pass) is honest and says nothing. Two thirds of it is grey, and five bodies (BLS, ORES, BTS, EIA, NASS) show as blank rows. A reader cannot tell a scanner that was refused from a publisher with nothing to show, and the page gives no reason.
- Holding the summary until the six `project_defined` checks are re-specified against published sources assumes the sources exist. The audit says two of the six have none to find (A9: no published construct; A10: the pre-JS floor is the project's). A hold would wait on a re-specification that cannot succeed on its own terms, against a January briefing.
- The instrument table makes the four states the finding. Every check is shown with its state and its source in one phrase; the grid under it shows only the `specified` checks, and a grey cell carries its reason class. "Could not measure" becomes a statement about the instrument and the access it was given, which is what it is.

A6 is the one exception the audit itself named (RESULT premise 7): it was set `invalid` on quotation coverage, and its rule may be sound. It is re-specified with its passages quoted and read again before the grid is built. If it comes back `specified`, the grid has five columns; if not, four. The task decides this from the reader's verdict, not from this note.

## 2. Prior art

The grounding is the argument-based view of validity: an instrument's report states the inferences its evidence supports and names the ones it does not, and a measurement that could not be made is reported as such, never folded into a failing score. Receipts from `seldon prior-art search`, run 2026-10-15 by an interactive Claude Code session, log `logs/dn014_add02_prior_art_search.log`; a search that fails is recorded as a failed search.

### External

- Kane, M. T. (2013). Validating the interpretations and uses of test scores. *Journal of Educational Measurement* 50(1). The interpretation/use argument: a validity claim is the chain of inferences the evidence supports, stated.
- AERA, APA, NCME (2014). *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*, chapter 1 (validity) and chapter 7 (supporting documentation): report the evidence for each intended interpretation and the limits of the instrument.
- Messick, S. (1989). Validity. In *Educational Measurement* (3rd ed.). Held in the corpus only through citing works (HELM; arXiv 2506.16697, 2505.08245) per the audit; recorded as such.
- Reporting guidelines that separate "not assessed" from "failed": the CONSORT 2010 statement's handling of missing outcomes, and the W3C WCAG-EM evaluation methodology's "not tested" and "not present" states, which are distinct from "fail". The WCAG-EM search failed in the audit; retried here.

What the library holds, and how:
- **Kane's argument-based view** is held only through arXiv 2506.16697. That work treats validity as "a single overarching interpretive argument". Kane (2013) itself is not held as a work.
- **The AERA, APA and NCME *Standards* (2014)** are held only through the same work's citation.
- **Messick (1989)** is held through arXiv 2506.16697 ("Building on Messick's (1989) unified treatment") and through HELM (arXiv 2211.09110), as the audit found.

What the library does not hold:
- **The CONSORT 2010 statement.** Its search returned only off-topic rows.
- **WCAG-EM.** It failed again, as it did in the audit.

Both are cited from knowledge.
