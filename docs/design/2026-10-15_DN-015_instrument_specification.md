# DN-015: The instrument specification: every check is a hypothesis with a published expected behavior, a stated test, and bounded claims

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** decided under the operator's standard of 2026-10-15 (quoted in `cc_tasks/2026-10-15_instrument_specification_audit.md`); written by the task that applies it (task db464d5f), Part A, before any entry is filled.
**Implements:** DN-014 as amended by DN-014 ADDENDUM 01 (the summary's page two), DN-001 (every check the report quotes reaches an admitted source), DN-005 §2.1 (the measurement specification layer of the framework).
**Amends:** DN-014 ADDENDUM 01 §2 on two points, named in §5 and §6 below: which checks page two may show, and the cell's states (three become four).

## 1. The standard

Every check in the harness is a hypothesis about a publisher. Before a result from it is shown to anyone, its entry answers four questions, in this order:

1. **Is it a valid question?** Does the construct the check measures matter to SI readiness, on published grounds (corpus documents, by id)?
2. **What is the expected behavior?** The behavior a ready publisher exhibits, as a standard or a paper defines it, not as this project does. A check with no published definition here is marked `project_defined`.
3. **What test is run, and why is it the recognized way to measure that behavior?** What the harness does, quoted from the rule's code, and the standard's own conformance test or a published method; where none exists, the entry says so.
4. **What does the result claim?** What a pass asserts, what a fail asserts, and what neither asserts, written so that a reader who knows the agency cannot read a cell as a claim the test did not make.

Nothing is asserted from a check whose entry is incomplete. The test is "correct", not "best known today": a check is not kept because nothing better exists.

This is construct validation in Messick's sense (the meaning of a score is the claim under test, and the evidence for it includes the content, the process that produced the response, and the consequences of using it) and Cronbach and Meehl's (a construct is defined by the network of claims it sits in, and a test is valid for a construct only as far as that network is evidenced). Field (2) is the content claim, field (3) the process claim, field (4) the use claim. The entry's shape follows the test-case specification of IEEE 829 (each test names its purpose, its inputs, its pass criterion and the basis for that criterion), and filling it before any result is shown is pre-registration: the hypothesis and the decision rule are fixed in writing before they are read against the data. The entries together are a codebook: one documented record per variable, as a survey's codebook documents each item (the DDI lineage, of which `ddi-cdi-1-0-specification` is in this corpus).

## 2. The entry

One file per check, `assessment/instrument/<rule_id>.yaml`, rendered as one table in `reports/instrument/INSTRUMENT.md` (and its PDF, by the same script). Fields:

- `question`: one sentence, the construct and why it matters to an SI client, with the corpus documents (by id) that establish it matters.
- `expected_behavior`: the behavior a ready publisher exhibits, with the standard or paper that defines it, cited by document id and section. If none exists, `source: none` and `state: project_defined`.
- `test`: what the harness does (client, request, what it reads, the decision rule), quoted from the rule's code, and why this is the recognized way to measure the behavior: the standard's own conformance clause, a published method, or the absence of one, stated.
- `claims`: what a pass asserts; what a fail asserts; what neither asserts.
- `confounds`: what else produces a fail (a firewall that refuses the client, a key wall, a server error) and how the harness distinguishes them, or that it does not.
- `branches`: each verdict branch of the rule's code (one per distinct reason the code can return), with the behavior that branch rewards or penalizes, its source, and its own state. A rule whose branches differ is split here, not averaged: a published branch keeps `specified`, an invented one is `project_defined`, and the record carries both.
- `state`: one of the four in §3.

A source is either a corpus document id (admitted in `corpus/manifest.json`) with a section, or a standards URL fetched through `scripts/fetch_allowlisted.py`, whose fetch-log line (URL, status, sha256) is copied into `assessment/instrument/standards_receipts.yaml`. Prose is never a source.

## 3. The states a check can hold

- **`specified`**: every field is filled, and every branch rewards or penalizes a behavior that a cited source defines. Only this state reaches page two.
- **`project_defined`**: at least one branch rewards or penalizes a behavior no source defines, or a behavior the cited source defines as something else. The check may stay in the harness and the detailed report, labelled; it is not shown as a finding about a publisher.
- **`invalid`**: the test measures something other than its question; that is, the behavior its decision rule enforces contradicts the source it cites (construct-irrelevant variance, in Messick's term), or the reader (§7) twice finds the expected behavior is the writer's rather than the source's. Its results are not shown anywhere as findings until a new rule version is specified.
- **`retired`**: the check is withdrawn (its rule version superseded, or its leg withdrawn by decision); its entry stays as the record of what it claimed.

A check's state is the weakest of its branches' states (`invalid` < `project_defined` < `specified`), because a cell can rest on any branch and the reader cannot see which.

## 4. Thresholds are part of the test, behaviors are part of the claim

A numeric parameter in a decision rule (a size floor, a visible-character floor, a link cap) is a property of the test. It is stated in field (3), its origin named (a source, or "chosen by this project"), and its error modes listed in `confounds`. It does not by itself make a check `project_defined`. A branch that rewards or penalizes a behavior (publishing a robots.txt, describing an API in OpenAPI, naming a licence by a recognised identifier) needs a source for that behavior, or it is `project_defined`.

## 5. Page two shows only `specified` checks

Amends DN-014 ADDENDUM 01 §2 ("Columns: the public-tier checks"). A column on page two is a check whose entry is `specified`. A check that is `project_defined`, `invalid` or `retired` comes off page two; it stays in the detailed report under its state. A declaration (a check that records what an agency declares rather than what a machine met) is never a page-two column, as ADDENDUM 01 already says; its entry states that it is a declaration.

## 6. The cell has four states

Amends DN-014 ADDENDUM 01 §2 ("Cells: one of three marks"). A page's verdict is **measured** when the harness observed the page and decided pass or fail, and **not measured** when the harness returned `error`: an access denial (the host refused the identified client), a page the scanner's own robots.txt compliance kept it from reading, an absence it could not confirm (the search for the object was incomplete), or a server or network error. DD-052 §6 already holds that `error` means the collector could not observe, never that the product failed; the v3 grid counted it as a fail, and this section corrects that.

- **Pass**: every one of the agency's data pages was measured and passed.
- **Fail**: every page was measured and failed.
- **Partial**: at least one page was measured and passed, and at least one was measured and failed.
- **Could not measure**: any other combination; that is, at least one page was not measured and the measured pages do not by themselves establish partial.

The cell makes a claim about every data page of the agency, so a page that was not measured leaves "every page passes" and "every page fails" both unestablished. This is the accessibility-conformance practice the three marks came from, completed: the VPAT's "Not Evaluated", and W3C's evaluation methodology (WCAG-EM), which makes a conformance claim only over what was evaluated; and it is the auditor's scope limitation (ISA 705, cited in `assessment/harness/scan/rules/_existence.py`): evidence that could not be obtained supports no opinion either way. The measured pages behind a "could not measure" cell stay on its locators.

## 7. The reader

Each entry is read, once, by a model of a different family from its writer (role `judge`), with three questions: does the expected behavior come from the cited source and not from the writer; could a reader who knows the agency misread a fail as a claim the test did not make; is any term undefined. A "no" on the first sends the entry back once; a second "no" sets `state: invalid` with the reader's reason. The reading is editorial review, not a measurement, and its lines are quoted in the task's RESULT.

## 8. What this does not decide

It does not rebuild `reports/summary/SUMMARY.pdf` (a follow-on task, registered after the operator reads the page-two verdict). It does not edit a rule: a `project_defined` or `invalid` branch is corrected by a new rule version under its own task, and the old version stays in `REGISTRY`, as every rule generation has.

### External

Construct validity and the unified view of validity are the frame (§1, §3).
