What this does not claim

One cycle, one client identity, one day, n = 26–40 depending on the leg, because a leg is denominated only by the surfaces it could actually observe. The rates here are a measurement of these 46 surfaces in cycle scan_2026-09-10_rj4, selected by a rule stated before any product was chosen, and they are not a score of any agency: there is no composite, no ranking, and the legs are not comparable to each other. 0 of 16 agencies contributed no surface at all, which is a property of the instrument as much as of them. The frame is 16 OMB-recognized statistical agencies and units plus 3 reference hosts, on 22 netlocs; the reference hosts are judged on tier-0 legs only and appear in no rate on this page (F6 shows them on their own). 7 agencies carry a host row and its probes and nothing else, because their products are not yet declared (BLS, BTS, DRSMSU, NAHMSAPHIS, NCES, ORES, SAMHSACBHS) — a thin row is a measurement, not a gap. 10 legs are at zero with an upper bound of 0.09, and a zero is not evidence of absence at these denominators. Where a leg's rate moved between cycles, F5 marks whether the RULE changed: on D4 (v2 → v3) it did, and a difference there is the instrument moving, not the host.

Excluded on purpose

  • any figure breaking errors out BY CLASS. The classes are now sound — scan/errors.py names every failure and the log carries one convention after the 266-observation overlay of cc_tasks/2026-09-07_scan_run_2.md §1.2 — and the counts are registered as Results per cycle; what is excluded is a CHART of them, because an error class is a fact about the instrument and this page is about the products
  • any agency composite, and any ranking of agencies
  • any language comparing one leg to another: they measure different constructs
  • the candidate indicator A12 in any fraction (DD-054)

Prior art the figures implement

  • Intervals. Wilson, E. B. (1927). Probable inference, the law of succession, and statistical inference. JASA 22:209. Brown, Cai & DasGupta (2001), Statist. Sci. 16:101, and Newcombe (1998), Stat. Med. 17:857, both recommend the score interval over Wald at small n and at 0 or n successes.
  • Zero counts. Hanley & Lippman-Hand (1983), JAMA 249:1743 — the rule of three: with 0 events in n, the 95 % upper bound is about 3/n. At these denominators that is about 0.13–0.14, which is what the Wilson bounds printed on F1 come to.
  • Encoding. Cleveland & McGill (1984), JASA 79:531, and Cleveland (1985), The Elements of Graphing Data: position along a common scale beats length. F1 is a dot-and-interval plot, not bars.
  • Matrix. Bertin (1967/1983), Semiology of Graphics, the reorderable matrix — deliberately NOT reordered here: reordering rows by pass count is a ranking of agencies.
  • Fractions. Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995), Psychol. Rev. 102:684: natural frequencies beside every rate, never a bare percentage.
  • Small multiples. Tufte (1983), The Visual Display of Quantitative Information.
