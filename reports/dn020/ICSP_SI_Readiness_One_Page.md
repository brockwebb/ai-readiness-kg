# Statistical-information readiness: the USAFacts ladder and where agencies stand

Prepared for the Interagency Council on Statistical Policy. Generated by `scripts/dn020_documents_build.py` from the framework record and the cycle of record.

USAFacts, a heavy consumer of federal data, has set out a framework for making government data ready for the programs the public now uses to reach it: three tracks, Agentic Access, Agentic Context and Agentic Evaluation, each a short ladder an agency climbs at its own pace, with three prerequisites that hold before any rung counts.
It is worth adopting because it names a destination in plain terms and lets each agency place itself and decide where to invest next, rather than asking for everything at once.

**Where the agencies stand today.** The readiness diagnostic has placed sixteen federal statistical agencies on the ladder from the outside, measuring the prerequisites and the lower rungs that a public request can reach.

*Figure three. Of sixteen agencies, eleven pass every measured check at Access I and one at Access II; on the Context track two pass at Context I and one at Context II. No agency is measured above these rungs, and the Evaluation track carries no check yet.*

**What an agency gets from the diagnostic.** For each track it receives its own rung and the single next thing that would raise it one rung, read from published criteria rather than asserted.
A check that could not be measured for an agency names its reason and never counts against it, so an agency sees where it stands and what to do next, not a grade.

The Evaluation track, where an agency would publish evaluation sets and benchmark results, carries no check yet: measuring it is a testing-and-evaluation programme, not a reading a public request can take, and it is shown empty rather than scored.
